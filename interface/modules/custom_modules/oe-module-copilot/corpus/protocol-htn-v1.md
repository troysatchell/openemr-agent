<!-- chunk: htn.diagnosis | source: protocol-htn-v1 | derived_from: AHA/ACC 2025 §Diagnosis; USPSTF Hypertension Screening -->
#### Diagnosis and confirmation

Hypertension is defined as an office blood pressure of **≥130/80 mm Hg** (BP
categories: normal <120/80; elevated 120–129/<80; stage 1 130–139/80–89; stage 2
≥140/90). Do not diagnose on a single elevated office reading: confirm with
**out-of-office measurement** — home BP monitoring (the preferred confirmatory
method) or ambulatory monitoring — to exclude white-coat effect and detect
masked hypertension. Use a validated device and correct cuff size; average
multiple seated readings taken after five minutes of rest.

<!-- chunk: htn.bp-target | source: protocol-htn-v1 | derived_from: AHA/ACC 2025 §Treatment Thresholds and Goals -->
#### Blood-pressure target

The treatment target is **<130/80 mm Hg** for most adults, including those with
diabetes or chronic kidney disease. Individualize the target upward only for
specific cases — institutionalized patients, limited life expectancy, or where
intensive lowering causes symptomatic hypotension — and note the rationale.
Older or frail patients warrant a target that balances benefit against the risk
of hypotension, electrolyte disturbance, and acute kidney injury.

<!-- chunk: htn.risk-assessment | source: protocol-htn-v1 | derived_from: AHA/ACC 2025 §Risk Assessment (PREVENT) -->
#### Cardiovascular risk assessment and when to start drug therapy

Estimate 10-year cardiovascular risk with the **PREVENT** equations (which
replace the pooled cohort equations and incorporate renal function, statin use,
and social drivers of health). Initiate **pharmacologic therapy in addition to
lifestyle change** for: stage 2 hypertension; or stage 1 hypertension with
clinical cardiovascular disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, or a PREVENT
10-year total CVD risk **≥7.5%**. Stage 1 hypertension below that risk threshold
is managed with lifestyle change first, with drug therapy if BP remains above
goal on follow-up.

<!-- chunk: htn.first-line-pharma | source: protocol-htn-v1 | derived_from: AHA/ACC 2025 §Pharmacologic Treatment; WHO 2021 pharmacological treatment -->
#### First-line pharmacologic therapy

First-line agents are **thiazide/thiazide-like diuretics, ACE inhibitors,
angiotensin-receptor blockers (ARBs), and dihydropyridine calcium-channel
blockers** — any of these classes is an acceptable initial choice. Do **not**
combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB. For most patients starting therapy,
consider initiating two agents (or a single-pill combination) when BP is ≥20/10
mm Hg above goal, to improve control and adherence. In Black adults without
heart failure or CKD, a CCB or thiazide is generally preferred as initial
monotherapy. Reassess and titrate at monthly intervals until goal is reached.

<!-- chunk: htn.lifestyle | source: protocol-htn-v1 | derived_from: AHA/ACC 2025 §Nonpharmacologic Interventions -->
#### Lifestyle modification

Recommend for all patients, at every stage: reduced dietary sodium; the DASH
dietary pattern; weight loss where overweight; increased physical activity;
moderation of alcohol; and smoking cessation. Lifestyle change is the sole
initial therapy for elevated BP and low-risk stage 1, and remains the foundation
alongside any pharmacologic therapy. Emphasize team-based care and home BP
monitoring to support engagement and titration.

<!-- chunk: htn.secondary-screening | source: protocol-htn-v1 | derived_from: AHA/ACC 2025 §Secondary Hypertension -->
#### Screening for secondary causes

Screen for **primary aldosteronism** in all patients with **stage 2 or
resistant hypertension** (BP above goal despite three agents including a
diuretic, or controlled on four or more), given its underdiagnosis. Consider
evaluation for other secondary causes — renal artery stenosis, obstructive sleep
apnea — where the clinical picture suggests them (e.g., resistant hypertension,
abrupt onset, or supporting symptoms).
