#### Recommendation — 10-year risk ≥10% (Grade B)

The USPSTF recommends that clinicians prescribe a statin for the primary
prevention of cardiovascular disease in adults aged **40 to 75 years** who have
**one or more cardiovascular risk factors** (dyslipidemia, diabetes,
hypertension, or smoking) and an estimated **10-year CVD event risk of 10% or
greater**. This is a **Grade B** recommendation (moderate net benefit).

<!-- chunk: uspstf-statin.recommendation-intermediate | source: uspstf-statin-2022 | derived_from: USPSTF 2022 (Grade C) -->
#### Recommendation — 10-year risk 7.5% to <10% (Grade C)

For adults aged **40 to 75 years** with one or more of the same risk factors and
an estimated **10-year CVD event risk of 7.5% to less than 10%**, the USPSTF
recommends that clinicians **selectively offer** a statin. The likelihood of
benefit is smaller in this group; the decision should reflect individual patient
circumstances and preferences. This is a **Grade C** recommendation (small net
benefit).

<!-- chunk: uspstf-statin.older-adults | source: uspstf-statin-2022 | derived_from: USPSTF 2022 (I statement) -->
#### Adults 76 years and older (Insufficient evidence)

The USPSTF concludes that the current evidence is **insufficient** to assess the
balance of benefits and harms of initiating statin use for the primary
prevention of CVD events and mortality in adults **aged 76 years or older**
(**I statement**).

<!-- chunk: uspstf-statin.population | source: uspstf-statin-2022 | derived_from: USPSTF 2022 §Population -->
#### Population and scope

This recommendation applies to adults aged 40 years or older **without a history
of known cardiovascular disease** (no prior myocardial infarction or stroke) and
**without signs or symptoms** of CVD. It does not apply to patients with very
high LDL cholesterol or known familial hypercholesterolemia, who are addressed
separately.
