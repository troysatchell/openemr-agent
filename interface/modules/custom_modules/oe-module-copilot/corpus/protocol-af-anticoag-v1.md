# `protocol-af-anticoag-v1` · Atrial Fibrillation — Anticoagulation Protocol

<!-- chunk: af.anticoag-indication | source: protocol-af-anticoag-v1 | derived_from: 2023 AF Guideline §Stroke Risk / Anticoagulation Indication -->
#### When to anticoagulate

Base the anticoagulation decision on **annual thromboembolic risk**, estimated
with a validated risk score such as **CHA₂DS₂-VASc**. Offer oral anticoagulation
when annual stroke risk is **≥2%** — equivalent to **CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥2 in men or
≥3 in women**. At very low risk (0 in men, 1 in women) anticoagulation is not
recommended. For intermediate risk, additional factors or an alternative
validated score (ATRIA, GARFIELD-AF) can refine the estimate and support shared
decision-making. Anticoagulation is independent of AF pattern — paroxysmal,
persistent, and permanent AF carry the same indication at a given risk.

<!-- chunk: af.doac-vs-warfarin | source: protocol-af-anticoag-v1 | derived_from: 2023 AF Guideline §Choice of Anticoagulant -->
#### DOAC versus warfarin

In DOAC-eligible patients, a **direct oral anticoagulant (apixaban, rivaroxaban,
dabigatran, or edoxaban) is preferred over warfarin**. The exceptions where
**warfarin is preferred** are **moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis** and a
**mechanical heart valve** — DOACs are not used in these patients. Do not use
antiplatelet therapy (e.g., aspirin) as a substitute for anticoagulation for
stroke prevention in AF.

<!-- chunk: af.bleeding-risk | source: protocol-af-anticoag-v1 | derived_from: 2023 AF Guideline §Bleeding Risk -->
#### Assessing bleeding risk

Assess bleeding risk, but **do not use a bleeding-risk score in isolation to
withhold or discontinue anticoagulation**. Use bleeding-risk assessment to
**identify and modify reversible risk factors** — uncontrolled hypertension,
labile INR, concurrent antiplatelet or NSAID use, excess alcohol — rather than
as a reason to deny therapy to an otherwise-indicated patient.

<!-- chunk: af.renal-hepatic-monitoring | source: protocol-af-anticoag-v1 | derived_from: 2023 AF Guideline §DOAC Dosing and Monitoring -->
#### Renal and hepatic monitoring, and dosing

Evaluate **renal and hepatic function before initiating a DOAC** and **at least
annually** thereafter (more often if function is impaired or changing), because
dosing and eligibility depend on it. Use the label-specified dose reductions for
renal impairment and other criteria; avoid inappropriate underdosing, which
reduces stroke protection without reliably lowering bleeding.

<!-- chunk: af.device-detected | source: protocol-af-anticoag-v1 | derived_from: 2023 AF Guideline §Device-Detected AF (AHRE) -->
#### Device-detected atrial high-rate episodes

For device-detected atrial high-rate episodes (AHRE — from pacemakers,
defibrillators, or wearables), weigh episode duration against stroke risk. It is
reasonable to initiate anticoagulation, within a shared-decision framework, for
an AHRE lasting **≥24 hours** in a patient with **CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥2 or equivalent
risk**. Shorter episodes and lower risk scores favor monitoring over
anticoagulation; individualize.
